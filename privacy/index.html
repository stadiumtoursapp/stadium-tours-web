Privacy policy

Draft, not legal advice. This plain-English draft will be reviewed before the app launches publicly.

Last updated November 27, 2026

Stadium Tours is an early preview app from The Away Game Co. This page explains what we keep, where it lives, and how to delete it.

The short version

You can use the app without an account. In guest mode, everything you add stays on your device and we never see it.

If you make an account, we keep your email address and the visits and memories you choose to back up, so you can get them back later. Your account and backup are private.

The Community tab (optional, accounts only) is different: what you post there — photos, captions, comments, and the community profile you choose to set up — can be seen by other signed-in members (only your approved followers if your account is private, which is the default). You can report posts, comments and people, and block or mute anyone.

We don’t sell your data, we don’t show ads, and we don’t use advertising or cross-app tracking tools.

The app counts which screens and buttons are used, anonymously, so we can improve it. It never includes your name, birth year, gender, favorite teams, home place, favorite snack, bio, email, journal, photos or location (only things like “finished setup step 3” or “skipped a step”), and you can switch it off in Profile (see “Anonymous usage stats”).

You can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app at any time (Profile → Account → Delete my account).

What stays on your device

Your profile (name, birth year, and any optional answers such as gender, favorite teams, home place, favorite concession item and bio), venue statuses, trip dates, journal entries, ratings, favorite foods, photos, saved places, map pins, road trips and settings are stored on your device (or in your browser on the web version).

Journal photos are never uploaded for backup, even with an account. (Photos you choose to post in the Community tab, or to show on your journey timeline for a visit, are uploaded; see below.) Deleting the app (or clearing the browser’s site data) deletes everything kept only on the device.

What we keep if you make an account

Account: your email address, a scrambled (hashed) password, when you signed up and last signed in, and when you confirmed you are 13 or older (from your birth year).

Profile: your name (display name) and birth year (both asked during profile setup; the birth year is used only to check you are 13 or older), and optional answers: how you describe yourself (gender), favorite teams, home state or province and country, favorite concession item, a short bio, and an optional username. A "private" setting is on by default. These profile answers are visible only to you (database rules let each account read only its own row). Your birth year can’t be changed after you set it.

Backup: only when you tap "Back up", we copy your venue statuses and trip dates, and your journal entries (date, game, opponent, score, seat, who you went with, favorite food, favorite moment, notes, rating, whether you’d recommend it, and how many photos the entry has, but not the photos themselves). A visit can also have just a month, just a year, or no date (“not sure”), which is backed up the same way.

Community (only if you set up a community profile): your name and profile photo, which any signed-in member can see so people can find you, even when your account is private; and what you choose to share: bio, interests, favorite teams, favorite venue, favorite concession, home state (optional) and the venues you’ve visited (only if you turn on “Share venues I’ve visited”). With a private account these details are visible only to followers you approve. Journey timeline (only if you turn on “Show my journey timeline”): for each past visit you have not hidden, its venue, date (or month, year, or “not sure”), rating, favorite food and favorite moment (cut to 280 characters). Notes, seat, opponent, who you went with and game result are never shared. Photos from a visit are shared only if you turn on “Also show my photos” for that visit. This follows your privacy setting like posts do: with a private account only approved followers see it, and blocked people never do. Turning the timeline off, or hiding a visit, removes it (and its photos) from your profile. Also stored: who you follow and who follows you, follow requests, communities you join, fan groups you start, posts (photos, caption, optional venue and community), likes, comments, saved posts (visible only to you), pins, blocks and mutes (visible only to you), and reports you send.

Photos you add to a visit are also shrunk on your device and re-saved (hidden data such as GPS location is removed) and kept only on the device. Community photos, including visit photos you choose to show on your profile: before upload, the app shrinks each photo on your device (longest side 1080 pixels, about 200 KB, plus a small thumbnail) and re-saves it, which removes hidden photo data such as GPS location and camera details. Photos are kept in private storage and shown through short-lived links only to people allowed to see the post.

Invite links: each account gets a short invite code and a link you can share. If someone opens your link and then makes an account, we store that they joined through you. You can see how many friends joined through your link, but not their names. They may see your display name on the welcome screen (“[Name] invited you to Stadium Tours”). This is not shown on public profiles.

Reports and moderation: when you report something, we store the reason, any details you add, what was reported and who reported it. The person reported is not told who reported them. The app owner (moderator) can read reports, see posts that were reported, approve or remove fan groups, and remove posts or comments that break the rules.

Blocking: when you block someone, neither of you can see the other’s posts, comments or profile details, follows between you end, and they can’t follow you again. They aren’t notified. Muting just hides someone’s posts from your feeds.

Basic technical records that our hosting provider keeps to run the service safely, such as IP addresses in security logs.

Anonymous usage stats

What we count: which screen was opened, which button was tapped, and simple actions such as “visit logged”, “entry saved”, “rating saved” or “theme changed”, with only safe details like a venue id (for example “cle”), the sport, a number of photos, or a star value. Each count also notes the platform (iPhone/iPad, Android or web) and the app version.

What we do NOT count or send: your name, nickname, email, username, bio, journal or memory text, favorite foods you typed, seats, opponents, photos, your location or map pins, search words, passwords, or anything from the Community tab such as captions, comments, community names, who you follow or who you reported (only counts like “post created with 2 photos” or “joined a team community”). Signing in does not link your account to these counts.

How it works: the counts are anonymous, tied only to a random number made on your device (no person profiles). We don’t use advertising IDs, session recordings, surveys, or location lookups. The counts are processed by PostHog (posthog.com), on its United States servers, on a free plan.

Your choice: Profile → Usage stats → “Share anonymous usage stats”. Turn it off and nothing more is sent, right away, and your choice is remembered. On the web, if your browser sends “Do Not Track”, stats start off. Some builds of the app have no stats built in at all.

Who stores it

Accounts and backups are stored with Supabase (supabase.com), our database and sign-in provider, on servers in the United States. Sign-in emails are sent through Supabase.

Each table is locked with database rules: private data (profile answers, backups, saved posts, blocks, mutes, reports, invite codes and who invited you) can be read only by its owner; community posts and details follow your privacy setting and blocks. Community photos are stored with Supabase Storage on the free plan.

Anonymous usage counts go to PostHog (see above). The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages. Maps use OpenStreetMap tiles (web) or Apple Maps / Google Maps (phones and tablets), which receive the map area you look at, as any map does.

How we use it

To run the app: to sign you in, to keep your backup, to send account emails (confirm your email, reset your password), and to keep the service secure. Anonymous usage counts are used only to see what works and what to fix. We don’t send marketing emails from the app.

Your choices

Stay a guest, or sign out at any time (your data on the device stays).

Delete a post or comment at any time. Make your account private or public, hide yourself from suggestions, or stop sharing your visited venues or your journey timeline in Community → Me → Edit profile (you can also hide single visits in the journal entry). The share pictures you make from your journey timeline are drawn on your device and are not uploaded.

Delete your account in the app. This deletes your account, profile, backup and everything you posted in the community (posts, photos, comments, likes, follows) right away. Copies in our provider’s routine backups expire on their normal schedule.

Ask us for a copy of your account data, or ask a question, at stadiumtoursapp@gmail.com.

Children

Accounts are for people 13 and older. During profile setup we ask for your birth year; if it shows you are under 13, we do not keep an account for you (an account made by mistake is deleted right away) and you can still use guest mode, where everything stays on your device. If you believe a child under 13 has made an account, email us and we’ll delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update the date above and show a notice in the app before the change matters to you.
